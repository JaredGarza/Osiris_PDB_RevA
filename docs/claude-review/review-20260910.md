# Review of Claude's PDB changes and cascaded simulation

Reviewed 10 September 2026. This review does not edit or stop Claude's simulation, the PDB schematic, or the Osiris PCB. Source code reviewed: `Simulation/Osiris_PDB_RevB_Cascade.cir`, last saved 16:31:11 local time. PDB connections were checked against a fresh KiCad netlist export in `current.net.xml`. Rev B PCB was read through KiCad's PCB API.

## Result

The two-stage power model is the right abstraction for studying startup, but the current deck is not a validated model of the revised hardware. Do not use its waveforms to finalize the fuse, TVS or U21 slew capacitor yet.

At the review snapshot, LTspice PID 21488 was running and consuming CPU. Its log had only the operating-point success and the SR=610 step, with no completed transient measurements. The raw file was about 647 MB; its last complete sample was at 0.339722 seconds in a requested 3-second transient. This demonstrates numerical progress, not successful completion. It is spending excessive samples around startup.

## Findings in priority order

### 1. U3 bypasses reverse-battery protection

Fresh netlist: U3 IN pin 8 and EN pin 5 connect directly to VBAT_FUSED, upstream of both pass MOSFETs. D1 is bidirectional SMAJ24CA. With a -16.8 V reversed battery, the TVS does not clamp at that magnitude, so U3 sees a voltage far below its -0.3 V input/enable absolute minimum. The main LTC4368 path being reverse protected does not protect this branch. A fuse is not a semiconductor reverse-voltage clamp.

Restore U3 to the protected output, or design a separately reverse-protected auxiliary branch, including input-discharge/output-backfeed behavior. The latter can preserve monitor state if required. Simulate that branch too when claiming reverse-input tolerance. [TI TPS7A16 datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps7a16.pdf).

### 2. The simulated gate network is the old topology

Deck lines 63-69 connect R4 between N004 and N002, C1 from N004 to ground, and the LTC4368 GATE terminal to N002, which is also both MOSFET gates. This is the old series-RC shunt arrangement. The revised schematic instead connects GATE to the C1/R4 junction (GATE_PIN), with R4 between that node and GATE_FET, plus D2 bypassing R4 during turn-off.

For the revised topology, X1's final terminal must be N004, MOSFET gates remain N002, and D2 must have anode N002/cathode N004. Verify the model pin order against the existing known pin definition. The deck currently has no D2 at all. Its turn-off/inrush results cannot validate the revised gate network. [ADI LTC4368 datasheet](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/LTC4368.pdf).

### 3. Fuse, TVS and auxiliary regulator are absent

No F1, D1, U3/C4 branch or D2 appears in the deck. It cannot test the added clamp, fuse coordination, or the U3 reverse-input problem. CPDB=11 uF is still numerically reasonable as current C9+C2; the comment calling it C4+C2 is stale. Fuse cold resistance alone is insufficient for opening-time qualification; use time-current/thermal behavior and defined fault-source impedance. TVS needs its pulse behavior, source/cable inductance and energy checked.

### 4. Constant-power load creates an artificial startup obstacle

`I = 23.07*V/(V*V+0.25)` draws 23.07 A at 0.5 V and about 7.48 A at 3 V. It is nearly full power long before the Osiris buck converters and Jetson can operate. That is not a realistic low-voltage startup model. A constant-power equivalent is useful after startup, but requires buck UVLO/hysteresis, a startup/load ramp, and a separate input-capacitor charging model. Keep the intended 7 W Jetson profile in the operating-load assumptions. Do not infer the whole board's demand from that module profile alone.

### 5. Behavioral U21 needs independent validation

The selected 28.1, 1.09 and 0.61 V/ms values do match TI's typical 12 V table. They are not fixed worst-case rates over all battery voltages, and the deck omits the corresponding turn-on delays. The BPASS equation is a very stiff feedback-controlled conductance, rather than a validated LM73100 model. Even a small NEN error below 1 adds a very large series resistance. The abrupt ramp/reset law adds further stiffness. Validate this model alone against capacitor-only startup and load steps before joining the two stages. Reverse blocking, fast-trip, thermal protection and delays are omitted, so fault-response claims need explicit limits. [TI LM73100 datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/lm7310.pdf).

### 6. Measurements currently misclassify normal startup as failure

Vpdb_min starts at 0.302 s and Nen_min at 0.310 s, while the upstream LTC4368 has about 32 ms turn-on delay after input qualification. Those windows include the intentionally-off period. Vsag can therefore report nearly the whole supply, and Nen_min can report zero even after a clean start. Trigger measurements from first valid output or use a post-startup settling window; count subsequent threshold crossings separately.

The short is 1 ohm for 20 us, not a sustained hard short. Simulation ends at 3 s, only 0.5 s after the 2.5 s fault. That does not cover the stated approximately 550 ms retry plus startup. Add separately defined brief-overload and sustained-short cases and extend the measurement interval. Save GATE_PIN, GATE_FET, VGS, shunt voltage, FAULT and RETRY along with terminal power/energy.

### 7. 2.22 A is a planning scenario, not an established maximum

My `calculate_power.py` explicitly assumes a 7 W Jetson target, total rail ceilings of 2.5 A at nominal 5.164 V and 2 A at 3.307 V, 85% conversion efficiency, 0.1 W auxiliary allowance and 75 milliohms aggregate path resistance. These yield 23.07 W before path loss and 2.22 A at nominal 10.56 V. They are not measured board loads or exhaustive part-by-part maxima, and do not bound arbitrary VIN-connected expansion modules, tolerances or startup.

Claude's promotion of that result to a confirmed design maximum, followed by removing PROVISIONAL from F1, is unsupported. A 4 A fuse remains a plausible candidate, but its final rating requires verified loads, ambient derating and fault coordination. Also, 23.07 W includes the auxiliary allowance; a refined model should account for that separately instead of applying it all behind U21.

### 8. Fuse I2t discrepancy is a document-version difference

Claude's local mirror really does list 4 A / 17 A2s and 5 A / 25 A2s. However, Littelfuse's manufacturer PDF revised 27 September 2024 lists 31 and 37 A2s respectively. The 37 figure was not simply a bad web extraction. Resolve the applicable specification before using either as a fuse model. Typical I2t is not a universal maximum fault let-through guarantee. [Current Littelfuse MINI datasheet](https://www.littelfuse.com/assetdocs/littelfuse-datasheet-297-mini32v?assetguid=42c9dd21-a88e-4328-8e67-2f832444faf1).

## Checks that agreed, and one PCB claim that did not

- Rev B's VIN-to-GND capacitor bank is eleven 10 uF capacitors: 110 uF, behind U21. Separating it from PDB output capacitance is correct. Our earlier 131 uF lump was only a preliminary aggregate estimate, not a validated two-stage startup model.
- U21 divider values give nominal UV on/off 7.914/7.189 V and OV trip/recover 27.700/25.161 V. PDB trips earlier nominally. This ordering alone does not validate battery discharge limits or guarantee transient protection of downstream absolute maximum ratings.
- Nominal capacitance does not make every simulated current peak an optimistic floor. Higher capacitance raises capacitive charging demand at fixed slew; lower effective capacitance can worsen droop and ringing. Sweep both ends rather than assuming one direction is always conservative.
- The PDB UV comparator senses its input divider, not PDBOUT. Output sag alone cannot trigger UV; source impedance must first pull the sensed input below threshold. At 14.8 V with 20 milliohms battery resistance, a 3.09 A load adds only about 62 mV input droop. U21 undervoltage dropout and upstream overcurrent are different failure mechanisms and need separate assertions.
- The Rev B 3.3 V net split and nineteen duplicated nonempty reference names are present. There is no component directly joining +3V3 and ST_LINK_T_PWR. The proposed cause is not proven by those facts. Ignoring that downstream implementation in a clearly bounded power-path simulation is reasonable; it is still a PCB release issue.
- **The claim that all 84 zones are keepouts is false.** KiCad reports 9 rule areas and 75 filled copper zones, including 24 GND, 11 VIN, 9 +5V0 and 9 ST_LINK_T_PWR zones. Searching for `net_name` is not a reliable parser for the file format. This does not certify internal-plane conversion or complete connectivity.

## Suggested next simulation pass

1. Make the deck match the current PDB and explicitly resolve U3 reverse protection.
2. Validate U21 alone with a stiff source and its 110 uF bank, then add staged low-power loads.
3. Join the stages and test 12/14.8/16.8 V, source-impedance and capacitance corners, hot-plug, OV, UV, load changes, sustained short and retry recovery.
4. Keep the fuse selection conditional until the resulting operating and fault envelopes are checked against the applicable manufacturer data. Scope exclusions should remain visible in every conclusion.
